JPG vs PNG vs WebP: Which Image Format Should You Use?

Published August 7, 2026 · Images Resizer Team

Choosing the right image format affects how sharp your images look, how fast your pages load, and even how well your site ranks in search. Here's a practical, no-jargon comparison of the three formats you'll actually use in 2026 — JPG, PNG and WebP — and simple rules for picking between them.

The 30-second answer

  • Photographs → JPG (or WebP for websites)
  • Logos, icons, screenshots, text graphics → PNG
  • Anything on a website where speed matters → WebP
  • Need transparency → PNG or WebP (JPG can't do it)

JPG: the universal photo format

JPG (or JPEG) has been the standard for photographs since the 1990s. It uses lossy compression — it throws away visual detail your eye is unlikely to notice — which makes files small. At 80–90% quality, a JPG photo looks essentially identical to the original at a fraction of the size.

Strengths: small files for photos, universally supported everywhere.
Weaknesses: no transparency; visible artifacts around sharp edges and text; quality degrades if you re-save repeatedly.

PNG: the lossless workhorse

PNG uses lossless compression — every pixel is preserved exactly. That makes it perfect for images with sharp edges, flat colors and text: logos, screenshots, charts, UI graphics. PNG also supports full alpha transparency, which is why virtually every logo on the web is a PNG.

Strengths: perfect quality, transparency support.
Weaknesses: photo files can be 5–10× larger than JPG.

WebP: the modern all-rounder

WebP, developed by Google, does both lossy and lossless compression and supports transparency. Its killer feature is efficiency: WebP files are typically 25–35% smaller than an equivalent-quality JPG and much smaller than PNG. Every modern browser has supported WebP for years, and it's now the default recommendation for web images.

Strengths: smallest files, transparency, great quality.
Weaknesses: a few very old apps and email clients still don't display it; not ideal for print workflows.

Practical scenarios

“I'm putting photos on my website”

Use WebP at 75–85% quality. Your pages will load faster, which improves user experience and SEO. Convert any image to WebP free here.

“I need a logo with a transparent background”

PNG. It's the safe, universal choice for transparency.

“I'm emailing photos”

JPG at 80% quality — universally viewable and small enough to attach. Compress your photos first to avoid bounced emails.

“I'm posting to Instagram or Facebook”

JPG. The platforms re-compress everything anyway; upload at the right dimensions for best results.
