How to Reduce Photo Size for Email

Published August 7, 2026 · Images Resizer Team

You attach a handful of photos, hit send, and the email bounces back: "message exceeds the size limit." Sound familiar? Gmail and Yahoo cap messages at 25MB, Outlook at 20MB — and with modern phone photos weighing 3–10MB each, just three or four pictures can break the limit. Here's how to shrink photos for email in under a minute, without making them look bad.

Email attachment limits at a glance

  • Gmail: 25MB total per message
  • Outlook / Hotmail: 20MB
  • Yahoo Mail: 25MB
  • Corporate mail servers: often just 10MB — the strictest common limit

Note that these are limits for the whole message, and attachments are encoded in a way that adds roughly 33% overhead. To be safe with a 10MB server limit, keep your total attachments under about 7MB.

Why phone photos are so huge

A modern phone shoots 12–50 megapixel images — around 4000–8000 pixels wide. A laptop screen is about 1500–2000 pixels wide, and an email preview pane is even smaller. In other words, you're sending 5–10× more pixels than the recipient can even display. Removing those unnecessary pixels is where nearly all the savings come from.

The 2-step shrink

Step 1: Resize to 1200px wide

Open the free image resizer, drop in your photo, and set the width to 1200 pixels (height adjusts automatically). This size looks great on any screen and prints acceptably at snapshot size. For photos that will only be viewed on a phone, 800px is plenty.

Step 2: Compress to taste

Run the resized photo through the image compressor at 75–85% quality. A typical 6MB phone photo lands at 150–350KB after these two steps — a 95% reduction with no visible quality loss at viewing size.

At ~250KB per photo, you can attach 80–100 photos before touching Gmail's limit, instead of four.

Common email photo scenarios

"The form says my photo must be under 1MB"

Job applications and government portals often cap single files at 1MB, 500KB or even 100KB. Resize to about 1000px, then move the quality slider until the live estimate is under the limit. Our guide to compressing images has a full table of quality settings.

"I need to email a scanned document"

Scans are usually huge PNGs. Convert them to JPG at 85% quality — text stays perfectly readable and the file often shrinks by 80%.

"The photos are for printing"

If the recipient will print at large sizes, don't downsize below ~2500px on the long edge. Instead compress lightly (90% quality) and send fewer photos per email, or split across two messages.

Privacy bonus: strip location data

Phone photos embed EXIF metadata, including the GPS location where the picture was taken. Because our resizer redraws the image in your browser, the resized copy contains no EXIF data — so emailing it doesn't reveal where you took it. And since processing happens entirely on your device, the photos themselves are never uploaded to any server.
