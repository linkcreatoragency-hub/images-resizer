Large image files slow down websites, exceed email attachment limits, and eat up storage. Compressing an image reduces its file size — often by 60–90% — while keeping it looking sharp. Smaller images mean faster page loads, better SEO rankings, and quicker sharing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I compress an image without losing quality?
Upload your image, set the quality slider between 70–85%, and download. At these settings the file size drops dramatically while the difference in quality is nearly invisible to the human eye.
How do I compress an image to a specific size like 100KB or 50KB?
Move the quality slider and watch the live estimated output size. Lower the quality until the estimate reaches your target — for example 100KB or 50KB — then click Compress & Download. Converting to WebP usually gives the smallest files.
Is compressing images here safe and private?
Yes. Compression runs entirely in your browser. Your photos are never uploaded to a server, so nobody else can ever see them.
What is the best format for small image file sizes?
WebP usually produces the smallest files at the same visual quality, followed by JPG. PNG is lossless and best for graphics with text or transparency, but produces larger files for photos.
